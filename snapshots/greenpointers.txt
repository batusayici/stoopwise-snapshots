Of course, Greenpointers are not a monolith. Supporters of the plan could be out there, just keeping very quiet. Like, Trappist monk quiet. An upcoming Brooklyn Community Board 1 meeting on September 15 will address these concerns (as well as other park developments). The board’s Parks and Waterfront Committee will meet at the Bushwick Inlet Park office (86 Kent Ave.) at 6 pm. The plot surrounding the project has developed since we last wrote about it. A recent Curbed piece revealed more about proposed concessionaire David Schmitt’s somewhat scattered history as a marina operator. The article also takes a closer look at the particular difficulties and regulations surrounding NYC Parks concessions projects. The decaying state of Transmitter Park’s current building requires an enormous amount of capital to renovate. NYC Parks told Curbed that Schmitt and Josh Cohen’s proposal was the only one that met their standards over three separate attempts to find a vendor. If it seems like the project just appeared out of nowhere, there might be a reason for that. It’s worth noting that the rules are extremely explicit about who is notified about concessions projects and when— the local community board and office of the Borough President. The process to select a vendor for city parks is guided by the Franchise and Concessions Review Committee (FCRC), an intra-agency group of representatives across several offices, operating under the Mayor’s Office of Contract Services. FCRC needs to contact the board and Borough President’s office at least a minimum of 40 days before the release of solicitation for a significant concession. The board and the Borough President don’t have the same automatic obligation to alert the community (as they are constantly inundated with these kinds of notices, such a practice could be unworkable). However, the rules also explicitly state that the board or Borough President’s office can request a consultation “on developing the scope of any solicitations for significant concessions relevant to each that are included in the Plan.” What did each office know and when? That’s a great line of questioning for the meeting’s attendees. Based on Parks documentation and conversations with sources close to the issue, we know they were both notified as far back as May 2025. We reached out to the office of Brooklyn Borough President Antonio Reynoso to get some additional info. “We can confirm that we have reviewed all testimony and will continue to review the proposal. We have no comment prior to the final vote on the Franchise and Concessions Review Committee,” a spokesperson told us. CB1 did not respond to our request by press time.

content.rendered: To commemorate the 25th anniversary of 9/11, Greenpointers is sharing a series of photos submitted by local resident Jennifer Formica, whose firsthand view documents a changing neighborhood: The morning of September 11, 2001. “I’m a photographer and college educator who has been photographing the view from my East Williamsburg studio window since I moved there in the summer of 1999. I moved into my rent-stabilized apartment when I was waiting tables in Manhattan, long before I began teaching college. The location was a quick shot on the L train and a place where many creative people were moving at the time. I made the back room of the apartment into my studio, which by chance had a clear view of Lower Manhattan. I’ve remained in the same apartment ever since, which is what made it possible for this unintentional project to continue for more than 25 years. For the first couple of years, the Twin Towers dominated the skyline, and I was mesmerized by them. I began photographing them casually, for personal memories rather than as art. On the morning of September 11, 2001, I photographed the skyline with my 35mm camera before the towers collapsed, returning to the window at dusk and the next morning. I had the film developed, but put the negatives away and didn’t really look at them again until the 10th anniversary of 9/11. As the construction of the new World Trade Center began to appear on the horizon, I became interested in the skyline again and started photographing more frequently. Over the years, I moved from film to digital photography, and the view became a record of new construction, gentrification, storms, the changing skyline, COVID-19, wildfire smoke from Canada and California, as well as the everyday beauty of sunsets and sunrises. There are now more than 500 photographs in the series. What began as personal memories became an ongoing visual record of more than 25 years of time and change. With the 25th anniversary of 9/11 approaching, the photographs capture more than a changing view. They capture memories, moments, and the sense of time constantly moving forward.” Summer 1999 Winter 2000 The morning after 9/11 September 15, 2012 October 31, 2012, after Hurricane Sandy May 20, 2018 June 29, 2019 June 7, 2023 during the Canadian wildfires. Tenants told Greenpointers that Rose Associates, the building’s management company, fired three of the building’s beloved doormen without notice and against the community’s wishes. These tenants have tried to engage Rose in a longer dialogue about what happened, but say the company has rebuffed their requests for a meeting. For several tenants, some of whom have lived in the building for years, the firing struck them as excessively cruel. Irving, Diamond, and Ganesh had become family; Irving was even recently featured in the popular TikTok series, Doorman Stories . “They had dog treats up there, they would remember details about people and just made the building feel so welcoming and so warm, which I think is hard for a big apartment building to feel that way generally in New York,” one resident, Indigo, told Greenpointers (for privacy reasons, we are identifying residents by first name only). “My kids—it’s New York City, small apartment—they were always in the lobby, and they formed great relationships, as I did, too, with the building staff, including the doormen,” said one former resident, Rachel. “I did their birthday parties at the little room in the building, and the doormen would come by with a little toy car and wish him happy birthday. Witnesses say the workers were fired on the spot, told to gather their belongings, and that they would not be welcome back in the building. Later that day, Rose sent the building an email about the situation, stating in part that: “These changes were made following a comprehensive review of operational needs and are intended to provide a stronger resident experience and a higher standard of service moving forward.” Tenants immediately sprang into action, firing up a WhatsApp chat and creating a website and petition asking for Rose to reinstate the staff. 220 residents have signed as of press time. Their level of care and service is a major reason many residents are so concerned about these changes,” said another. Tenants papered the building with the petition, which eventually got removed. They also repeatedly asked Rose to host a meeting in order to get more answers about the situation. Eventually, Rose followed up in an email, stating “that management acted in response to complaints they received from building residents,” and that they could not comment further due to confidentiality. Considering the building’s united front, that claim felt very hard for tenants to take seriously. “You guys have already shown that you don’t really care about the residents, but now you think that we’ll believe that you fired three of the staff members due to a couple of mysterious complaints,” said Indigo “I’ve never dealt with people like this in my life. What’s more, if the ostensible goal is to improve service, residents find the replacement staff to be a noticeable downgrade, especially one particular front desk staffer. “She just has a very, very nasty attitude,” said one tenant. Another claims this staffer even stole residents’ packages, calling the new team “horrible.” Many New York City building staff are unionized through 32BJ SEIU, but staff at 50 North 5th were contracted through third-party firm QCS. The decision to fire the trio raises some questions, as it appears that Rose is still working with QCS. Residents speculate that Irving, Diamond, and Ganesh were being paid at a higher rate than Rose wanted to offer, leading them to clear house with less experienced (and cheaper) staff. Though the firing was abrupt, the writing might have been on the wall earlier. Residents say Ganesh is religiously observant and that previously, his schedule reflected that without issue. That changed once Rose took over. “They would interrupt his prayer time and then not have them eat lunch for, like, the whole day,” said V. The termination agreement offered to the trio includes a non-disparagement clause. By the nature of this story moving forward, it doesn’t appear that any of them took up Rose’s offer. Rose Associates took over control of the building in August 2025. In response to these claims, a Rose spokesperson sent the following statement: “With respect to recent staffing changes at 50 N 5th, we can confirm that the changes were the result of complaints received from building residents. The staff members involved were employed by a third-party vendor that provides personnel solutions to residential properties. Rose Associates did not employ these individuals, did not manage their day-to-day schedules, and did not terminate their employment. Should residents have concerns regarding current building staff, Rose Associates is always available to receive that feedback and forward it directly to the appropriate employer. Out of respect for individual privacy, Rose Associates will always keep personnel matters confidential.” However, this appears to contradict what Rose told residents in the August 10 email. In that email, Rose explicitly states that, “Ownership and Management made the decision to adjust front desk staffing” and that “[a]s a rental community, the management and operation of the property, including staffing decisions, vendor selections, policies, procedures, and operational practices, are the responsibility of Ownership and Management.” A Rose rep did not respond to our follow up question about the discrepancy. We were also unable to reach QCS for comment about the building’s employment structure. In a recent Biznow article, sponsored by Rose , the management company positions itself as expanding their footprint across the Northeast. The article states that Rose “has more than 40,000 units under management” and is “one of the largest firms in the region.” “Renters can get four vanilla walls anywhere, there are lots of beautiful apartments with great amenities, but what makes the difference is customer service and engagement,” a Rose rep told Biznow. “Everyone is looking for management they can depend on.” The residents at 50 North 5th Street feel like they’re still looking.
